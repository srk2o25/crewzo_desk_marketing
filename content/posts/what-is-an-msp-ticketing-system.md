## What does an MSP ticketing system do?

An MSP ticketing system records every support request from every client company as a ticket. Each ticket has an owner, a priority, a status and a deadline, and the full history stays attached. Technicians see one queue, while each client's data stays separate.

The point is that nothing depends on one technician's memory. A request that arrives on a busy afternoon is still tracked, assigned and measured.

## How is it different from a normal help desk?

A normal help desk supports one organisation. An MSP supports many, and each client has its own people, rules and expectations. The ticketing system has to hold all of that at once.

The differences that matter most:

| Need | Normal help desk | MSP ticketing system |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who raises tickets | Employees of one company | Staff of many client companies |
| SLA targets | One set for everyone | A separate policy per client |
| Business hours | One calendar | A calendar and time zone per client |
| Reporting | Team totals | Per-client response and resolution times |

## Why does the SLA need to be set per client?

Clients buy different service levels. One client may be covered around the clock, another only from 9 to 5 in its own time zone. If a single SLA applies to everyone, the clock runs in the wrong hours for some clients and breach alerts become noise.

A per-client SLA starts and stops the clock using that client's own hours, so an alert means a real deadline is at risk. Crewzo Desk is built around this: each client gets its own [SLA policy](/), with its own business hours and time zone.

## Which features should an MSP look for?

Start with the features that remove daily friction, then add the rest as the business grows.

- **Client separation:** tickets, contacts and SLA terms kept apart for each client.
- **One shared queue:** technicians work across clients without switching tools.
- **SLA tracking and early alerts:** a warning before a deadline passes, not after.
- **Intake where clients already write:** email, a portal, or chat such as Microsoft Teams.
- **Time tracking and reports:** to see which clients take the most effort.

## Do small MSPs need a full PSA?

Not always. A small MSP that already has billing and monitoring tools it is happy with may only need a ticketing system that handles clients and SLAs well. A full PSA makes sense when contracts, billing and projects also need to live in one place.
